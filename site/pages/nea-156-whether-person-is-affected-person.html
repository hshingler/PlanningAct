NEA §156 — Whether person is affected person

PageprovisionNEA2026
  • (1) For the purpose of section 154(2)﻿(b)﻿(ii) and (4),—
    • (a) a person is an affected person if the permit authority decides that—
      • (i) the activity’s adverse effects on the person are more than minor; or
      • (ii) the activity’s adverse effects on the persons within a management unit are more than minor; but
    • (b) a person is not an affected person if—
      • (i) the person has given, and not withdrawn, approval for the proposed activity in a written notice received by the permit authority before the authority has decided whether there are any affected persons; or
      • (ii) the permit authority is satisfied that it is unreasonable in the circumstances for the applicant to seek the person’s written approval.
  • (2) When assessing whether an activity’s adverse effects on a person or the persons within a management unit are more than minor, the permit authority—
    • (a) must disregard an adverse effect of the activity on the person or the persons within a management unit if a rule in a natural environment plan or a national rule permits an activity with that effect, subject to subsection (3); and
      NEP
    • (b) if the activity is a restricted discretionary activity, must disregard an adverse effect of the activity on the person or the persons within a management unit if the effect does not relate to a matter for which a rule in a natural environment plan or a national rule has reserved discretion; and
      NEP
    • (c) if the activity is a controlled activity, must disregard an adverse effect of the activity on the person or the persons within a management unit if the effect does not relate to a matter for which a rule in a natural environment plan or a national rule has reserved control; and
      NEP
    • (d) must have regard to every relevant statutory acknowledgement.
  • (3) If the activity is a natural resource use activity, the permit authority may, in its discretion, consider any adverse effect of the activity on natural resources or people regardless of whether a rule in a natural environment plan or national rule permits an activity with that effect.
    NEP
