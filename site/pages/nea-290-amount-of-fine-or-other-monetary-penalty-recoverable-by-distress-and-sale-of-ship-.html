NEA §290 — Amount of fine or other monetary penalty recoverable by distress and sale of ship or from agent

PageprovisionNEA2026
  • (1) The court may order that the amount of an unpaid fine be levied by distress and sale of a ship and its equipment if—
    • (a) the master or owner of the ship is convicted of an offence against section 286 in respect of any contravention of section 25, 26, or 27; and
    • (b) any fine or other monetary penalty imposed by a court under section 288 or clause 30 or 31 of Schedule 8 of the Planning Act 2026, in respect of that offence is not paid on time.
  • (2) Without limiting subsection (1), subsection (3) applies if a master or an owner of a ship—
    • (a) is convicted of an offence against section 286 in respect of any contravention of section 25, 26, or 27; and
    • (b) fails to pay the full amount of any fine or other monetary penalty imposed by the court under section 288 or clause 30 or 31 of Schedule 8 of the Planning Act 2026.
  • (3) The agent of the ship is civilly liable to pay to the Crown or, if the proceedings in relation to the offence were commenced by or on behalf of a local authority, to that local authority, any amount of that fine or monetary penalty that remains unpaid and the Crown or that local authority may recover that amount from that agent as a debt.
  • (4) For the purpose of subsection (2), any proceedings in relation to the offence that were commenced by or on behalf of a local authority include any proceedings in which the EPA was assisting the local authority (see section 274(b)).
  • (5) An agent of a ship who, under this section, pays the whole or part of any fine or other monetary penalty imposed on the master or owner of the ship is entitled—
    • (a) to recover the amount paid from that master or owner as a debt; or
    • (b) to deduct that amount out of, or from, any money that is or becomes payable by that agent to that master or owner.
  • (6) An amount paid by the agent under this section is deemed to be a disbursement made on account of the ship for the purposes of section 4(1)﻿(p) of the Admiralty Act 1973.
  • (7) The District Court has jurisdiction to hear and determine proceedings for the recovery, in accordance with this section, of any money from any agent or master or owner of a ship, whatever the amount of money involved.
  • (8) This section applies despite any legislation or rule of law to the contrary.
