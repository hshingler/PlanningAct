NEA §321 — Regulations

PageprovisionNEA2026
  • (1) The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, make regulations for all or any of the following purposes:
    • (1) providing for anything this Act says may or must be provided for by regulations:
    • (2) prescribing, for the purposes of any provision of this Act that requires a thing to be done in the manner prescribed by regulations, the manner in which the thing must be done, including prescribing—
      • (a) by whom, where, and how the thing must be done:
      • (b) by when the thing must be done, including by setting a deadline:
      • (c) the form that must be used for doing the thing:
      • (d) information or other evidence that must be provided in connection with the thing, including requirements that the information or evidence must comply with:
      • (e) requirements for serving notice in connection with the thing:
    • (3) prescribing the manner or content of applications, notices, certificates, or any other documentation or information required under this Act, including the service of notices:
    • (4) prescribing the form to be used—
      • (a) for any application, notice, certificate or other document or information required under this Act:
      • (b) for the purposes of any provision in this Act that requires a thing to be in the form prescribed by regulations:
    • (5) authorising the chief executive of the Ministry to prescribe any form under paragraph (2)﻿(c) or (4):
    • (6) requiring the payment of fees or charges in connection with—
      • (a) an application, notice, or request provided for under this Act:
      • (b) the performance or exercise of any function, power, or duty under this Act:
    • (7) prescribing any of the following in relation to fees or charges required under paragraph (6):
      • (a) the fee or charge payable or the methods for calculating fees or charges:
      • (b) the persons liable to pay the fees or charges:
      • (c) the persons to whom the fees or charges must be paid:
      • (d) when the fees or charges must be paid:
      • (e) the circumstances in which the fees or charges may be refunded, remitted, or waived (wholly or partly):
    • (8) prescribing the amount, the methods for calculating the amount, and the circumstances and manner in which holders of natural resource permits are liable to pay for the following:
      • (a) the occupation of the coastal marine area that is within the common marine and coastal area:
      • (b) the occupation of the bed of any river or lake that is land of the Crown:
      • (c) the extraction of any sand, shingle, shell, and other natural materials from an area described in subparagraph (a) or (b):
      • (d) the use of geothermal energy:
    • (9) specifying the offences under this Act (including offences prescribed under paragraph (25)) that constitute infringement offences and prescribing infringement fees for those offences—
      • (a) not exceeding $2,000, in the case of a natural person:
      • (b) not exceeding $4,000, in the case of a person other than a natural person:
      • (c) not exceeding $100 per stock unit for each infringement offence prescribed under paragraph (25) that is differentiated on the basis of the number of stock units, to a maximum fee of—
        • (i) $2,000 for each infringement offence in the case of a natural person; and
        • (ii) $4,000 for each infringement offence in the case of a person other than a natural person:
    • (10) requiring the holders of water permits, discharge permits, coastal permits, or land use permits granted for any activity that would otherwise contravene section 22 to keep records for any purpose under this Act, and prescribing the nature of records, information, and returns, and the form, manner, and times in or at which they shall be kept or furnished:
    • (11) prescribing the form and content (including conditions) of water permits and discharge permits:
    • (12) specifying requirements for the manner in which a person must comply with section 10(2), including where, how, and when the person must comply with that section:
    • (13) prescribing exemptions from any provision of section 24, either absolutely or subject to any prescribed conditions, and either generally or specifically or in relation to particular descriptions of contaminants or to the discharge of contaminants in particular circumstances or from particular sources, or in relation to any area of land, air, or water specified in regulations:
    • (14) prescribing standards, methods, and other requirements for the use of data or digital technology in any document, process, or other matter under this Act:
    • (15) doing anything that a national rule may do under subpart 3 of Part 2 (for example, classifying activities):
    • (16) deeming to be included in any natural environment plan or proposed natural environment plan rules that do any of the following:
      NEP
      • (a) classify activities to which section 25 applies as controlled activities, restricted discretionary activities, discretionary activities, or prohibited activities:
      • (b) specify criteria that must be considered when a permit authority is considering an application for a coastal permit to do something that would otherwise contravene section 25, an application to change or cancel a condition of such a coastal permit, or a review of the conditions of such a coastal permit:
    • (17) prescribing any substance to be a harmful substance for the purposes of section 3:
    • (18) prescribing any waste or other matter to be toxic or hazardous waste for the purposes of section 27:
    • (19) without limiting paragraph (10), in relation to any coastal permit to do something that otherwise would contravene section 25,—
      • (a) requiring the holder of the coastal permit to keep records; and
      • (b) requiring the holder of the coastal permit to provide to any specified agency or local authority information in relation to any activity carried out under the coastal permit; and
      • (c) prescribing the nature of the records or information and the form, manner, and times in or at which they must be kept or provided:
    • (20) in relation to a discharge to which section 26 applies (which regulations may describe by referring to the circumstances, quantities, components, or sources of the discharge),—
      • (a) prohibiting or permitting the discharge; or
      • (b) controlling the discharge by prescribing conditions or limitations, or by other means; or
      • (c) prohibiting or permitting, with or without conditions, the making of a rule; or
      • (d) prohibiting or permitting, with or without conditions, the granting of a natural resource permit:
    • (21) prescribing any operations of a ship, aircraft, or offshore installation as a normal operation for the purposes of the definition of dumping in section 3:
    • (22) providing for discounts on administrative fees imposed under section 331 when local authorities do not meet time limits set under this Act:
    • (23) requiring local authorities to provide information gathered under sections 231 and 232 to the Minister, and prescribing the content of the information to be provided and the manner in which, and time limits by which, it must be provided:
    • (24) prescribing measures for the purpose of excluding stock from water bodies, estuaries, coastal lakes and lagoons, and the margins of those water bodies, estuaries, and coastal lakes and lagoons, including regulations that—
      • (a) apply generally in relation to stock or to specified kinds of stock (for example, dairy cattle):
      • (b) apply generally in relation to water bodies, estuaries, coastal lakes and lagoons, and their margins, or to specified kinds of water bodies, estuaries, coastal lakes and lagoons, and their margins:
      • (c) apply different measures to different kinds of stock or to different kinds of water bodies, estuaries, coastal lakes and lagoons, and their margins:
      • (d) prescribe technical requirements for the purposes of the regulations:
    • (25) prescribing infringement offences for the contravention of, or non-compliance with, any regulations made under paragraph (24):
    • (26) prescribing requirements that apply to the use of models (being simplified representations of systems, for example, farms, catchments, and regions) under this Act by—
      • (a) local authorities:
      • (b) the holders of natural resource permits:
      • (c) other persons:
    • (27) providing for anything incidental that is necessary for carrying out, or giving full effect to, this Act.
  • (2) Regulations made under subsection (1)—
    • (a) may apply generally; or
    • (b) may apply or be applied from time to time by the Minister by notice—
      • (i) within any specified district or region of any local authority or within any specified part of New Zealand:
      • (ii) to any specified class or classes of persons.
  • (3) Regulations may be made under subsection (1)﻿(12) only on the Minister’s recommendation after—
    • (a) consulting any relevant post-settlement governance entity, or ngā hapū o Ngāti Porou, on the proposed regulations; and
    • (b) being satisfied that the manner of compliance specified in the regulations will, to the greatest extent possible under this Act, have an effect that is the same as, or equivalent to, the effect that the relevant redress or arrangement had under the Resource Management Act 1991.
  • (4) Regulations may be made under subsection (1)﻿(15) only on the Minister’s recommendation after being satisfied that the regulations are necessary or desirable for either or both of the following:
    • (a) to implement New Zealand's obligations under any international convention, protocol, or agreement to which New Zealand is a party:
    • (b) to enable New Zealand to become a party to any international convention, protocol, or agreement.
  • (5) The Minister must not recommend the making of regulations under subsection (1)﻿(16), (17), (18), or (19) unless, after having consulted the Minister of Transport and the Minister of Conservation, the Minister is of the opinion that—
    • (a) it is necessary or desirable to do so for 1 or more of the following purposes:
      • (i) to implement New Zealand's obligations under any international convention, protocol, or agreement relating to the protection of the marine environment and to which New Zealand is a party:
      • (ii) to enable New Zealand to become a party to any international convention, protocol, or agreement relating to the protection of the marine environment:
      • (iii) to implement international practices or standards relating to the protection of the marine environment that may, from time to time, be recommended by the International Maritime Organization; or
    • (b) it is not inconsistent with any such purpose to do so.
  • (6) The Minister may amend any schedule of any regulations made under subsection (1)﻿(17) or (18) by omitting or inserting the names or a description of waste or other matter or harmful substance to make that schedule comply with the provisions of an international convention relating to the pollution of the marine environment.
  • (7) Regulations made under subsection (1)﻿(20) may apply generally to rules or natural resource permits, or to rules or natural resource permits made by the permit authorities specified in regulations.
  • (8) Regulations made under subsection (1)﻿(24) or (25) may specify—
    • (a) that rules inconsistent with those regulations be withdrawn or amended—
      • (i) to the extent necessary to remove the inconsistency; and
      • (ii) as soon as practicable after the date on which the regulations come into force; but
      • (iii) without using any of the processes under Schedule 3 of the Planning Act 2026 for changing a plan or proposed plan; and
    • (b) in relation to a rule made before the commencement of the regulations,—
      • (i) the extent to which a matter that the regulations apply to continues to have effect; or
      • (ii) the period for which a matter that the regulations apply to continues to have effect.
  • (9) If regulations specify a matter under subsection (8), the local authorities concerned must publicly notify that the rules have been withdrawn or amended not later than 5 working days after they are withdrawn or amended.
  • (10) Regulations may be made under subsection (1)﻿(1) that prescribe infrastructure as long-lived infrastructure for the purposes of paragraph (h) of the definition of long-lived infrastructure in section 3, only on the Minister's recommendation after being satisfied that the infrastructure—
    • (a) has an expected life span of 50 years; and
    • (b) is suitable for a permit duration of 35 years; and
    • (c) benefits the public.
  • (11) Regulations made under subsection (1) may incorporate material by reference. Section 91 applies as if a reference to national standards in that section were a reference to regulations made under subsection (1).
  • (12) The following are secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements):
    • (a) regulations made under subsection (1):
    • (b) a notice made under subsection (2)﻿(b):
    • (c) an amendment made under subsection (6).
