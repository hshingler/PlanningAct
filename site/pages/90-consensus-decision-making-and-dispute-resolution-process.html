§90 — Consensus decision-making and dispute resolution process

PagePA2026provision
Linked references reflect saved graph links. Unlinked references are candidate title matches and may need review. This prototype is not a verified legal publication.
  • (1) The local authorities of a region must do all things reasonably practicable to achieve consensus with each other when making a decision on any matter under this Act relating to the preparation, change, or implementation of a regional spatial plan.
  • (2) If 2 or more local authorities of a region are unable to achieve a consensus using their respective procedures on a decision to which subsection (1) applies, they must follow the dispute resolution processes prescribed by regulations.
  • (3) The members of a spatial plan committee must do all things reasonably practic- able to achieve consensus when making a decision on any matter under this Act relating to the preparation, change, or implementation of a regional spatial plan.
  • (4) If the chairperson of a spatial plan committee determines that a spatial plan committee is unable to achieve consensus on a decision to which subsection (3) applies, the spatial plan committee must follow the dispute resolution process prescribed by regulations.
  • (5) In this section and section 91, consensus means—
    • (a) unanimity; or
    • (b) general agreement characterised by the absence of sustained opposition on any substantial issue.
