The Funnel Folly

The new planning system in New Zealand is described as a funnel, touting the promise of more centralised decision-making to support fewer needless debates, thereby reducing litigation and process costs. National consistency, delivered via the funnel, is presented as both the answer and the architecture. But to use it as a proxy for certainty and efficiency is a rather big claim.

'The funnel' has an out-of-zeitgeist, dystopic quality about it. Totally unrelated, but Hieronymus Bosch's funnel-wearing figures share similarly uncanny imaginative connections with this rather dull apparatus. The funnel features across Bosch's works as a curious headpiece, adorning physicians, priests, nuns and other creatures.

The funnel does not seem to be used as it should. Authoritative figures wearing funnel fascinators, touting empty wisdom. It's all for show; all to deceive the gullible. But over time, body-object, man-funnel... an uncanny being with a life of its own. Those who once deceived become gullible themselves. A tool they formed is now beyond themselves. Those wearing the funnel, falsifying their own authority.

These strange funnel menageries feel deeply rooted in the silly creatures of medieval imagination. Perhaps our funnel and Bosch's are not entirely unrelated after all, given the planning system's own inheritance of English legal and institutional thinking.

All to say, no one wants to be an accomplice to this funnel folly. But here we are. Be wary of the stone of madness hardening in your head.
